What a VRFB costs, and why

A lithium-ion system is mostly cells. A VRFB is roughly a third stacks, a third electrolyte and a third everything else, and the split moves with duration: at 2 hours the stacks dominate; at 10 hours the electrolyte does.[5][14]

  • Stacks and power electronics scale with kilowatts. Membrane and bipolar plates are the costliest stack parts.[14]
  • Electrolyte scales with kilowatt-hours and tracks the vanadium pentoxide (V₂O₅) price, which has swung from about $5 to over $25 per pound within a decade.[17]
  • Tanks, pumps, piping, controls, installation scale with both.

The U.S. Department of Energy's 2022 assessment puts installed costs for utility-scale vanadium flow systems in the low hundreds of dollars per kWh for 10-hour durations, with electrolyte the largest single line item; the exact figures depend on year, size and vanadium price, so use the calculator's inputs as editable assumptions rather than quotes.[5]

Levelised cost of storage calculator

The levelised cost of storage (LCOS) spreads every lifetime cost over every megawatt-hour the battery will ever discharge, discounted to today. It is the fairest single number for comparing technologies with different lifetimes.[6][13]

LCOS = [ CAPEX + Σt (O&Mt + chargingt + replacementst) / (1+r)t − residual / (1+r)N ] / Σt Edischarged,t / (1+r)t

Vanadium mass per kWh is derived from the concentration and SOC window at a 1.35 V nominal cell voltage; at 1.6 M and 80 % window that is about 6 kg of V₂O₅ per kWh. Residual value reflects that vanadium electrolyte can be recovered or re-leased at end of life.[14][17] The lithium-ion line uses a 90 % usable window and no residual value.

The vanadium question

About 85 % of vanadium goes into steel as a strengthening additive, so its price follows the steel cycle rather than the battery market. The 2018 spike to above $25 per pound of V₂O₅ nearly doubled electrolyte costs; prices in the mid-2020s have been in the single digits per pound.[17]

Two responses have emerged. Electrolyte leasing moves the vanadium off the project's balance sheet: the supplier retains ownership and recovers the metal at end of life, converting a capital cost into an operating fee.[14] Alternative electrolytes (mixed-acid, higher concentration) squeeze more energy from each kilogram.[3][18]

Advanced · sensitivity

Because electrolyte is proportional to energy, LCOS sensitivity to vanadium price grows with duration. Doubling V₂O₅ from $10 to $20 per kg raises the calculator's default 8-hour LCOS by roughly a fifth; at 4 hours the effect is about half that. Stack cost sensitivity works the other way. This is why VRFB vendors target 6–12 hour applications while lithium-ion dominates 1–4 hours.[5]
