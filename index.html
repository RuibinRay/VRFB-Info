01 · Power and energy, separated

Power lives in the stack. Energy lives in the tanks.

Dozens of cells are pressed between two steel end plates. Their total electrode area sets how many kilowatts the battery can deliver. The two tanks beside it hold the electrolyte, and their volume alone sets how many hours it can run. Need more hours? Buy bigger tanks, not more stacks.[2]

02 · Anatomy of a cell

Eleven layers, one cell.

End plate, copper current collector, flow frame, graphite bipolar plate, carbon-felt electrode, ion-exchange membrane, and the same again in mirror. Every neighbouring cell shares a bipolar plate, so a stack of 40 cells is 40 membranes and 41 plates.[4]

03 · Going microscopic

Into the felt.

The electrode is a mat of carbon fibres a few millimetres thick, each fibre about 10 µm across. Electrolyte seeps through the pores and the reaction happens on the fibre surface, where ions meet electrons.[10]

05 · Follow the flow

A serpentine for every cell.

Pumps push electrolyte through channels machined into the bipolar plate so that it sweeps evenly across the felt. Engineers run the pumps at 5–10× the stoichiometric minimum: too little flow starves the fibres of vanadium, too much wastes pump power.[14]

06 · Back together

Cells in series add volts.

Tie rods clamp the layers to about 1 MPa so the felt is compressed and the gaskets seal. Forty cells at 1.3 V make a 52 V stack; a container of stacks makes a megawatt.[4]

07 · Energy in the tanks

Watch the colour change as it charges.

Nothing is plated, nothing is consumed. Charging simply converts V³⁺ to V²⁺ in the negative tank (green to violet) and VO²⁺ to VO₂⁺ in the positive tank (blue to yellow). The same electrolyte has cycled more than 20,000 times in field systems.[5][8]

08 · From the solar farm to the grid

Midday sun, delivered at dinner time.

A flow battery sized for 4–12 hours soaks up the midday solar peak and releases it through the evening ramp. The world's largest, in Dalian, China, stores 400 MWh and delivers 100 MW.[7]

Scientifically verified

Standard potentials, concentrations, efficiencies and costs are taken from peer-reviewed papers and national-laboratory reports, listed with DOIs on the sources page. Where a figure is an estimate or an assumption, the page says so.

Built as one experience

The 3D journey is driven entirely by your scroll position, so it plays forwards and backwards. The model geometry is procedural, built in Three.js from the reference renders, and the colours of the tanks follow the real oxidation-state colours of vanadium.

Open and portable

Plain HTML, CSS and JavaScript with no build step. Fork the repository, edit the data files, and host it on GitHub Pages.
